Clear Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Before we pick up a wrench, we figure out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check your system's age and tonnage, the refrigerant charge, the heat exchanger and coils, and the condition of your ductwork. Then you get a straightforward recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is swapping a worn capacitor or replacing the whole system. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

The problem with HVAC failures is that most of them develop out of sight. A pinhole leak in the evaporator coil slowly drains refrigerant, the blower bearings wear down a little at a time, and a weak capacitor holds on until the first hot afternoon, when the compressor won't start. If your bills are climbing, airflow drops, or ice forms on the lines, have the system checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most air conditioners and furnaces last 12 to 18 years, and a planned replacement is always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency swap during a heat wave or cold snap. Our technicians know the climate demands common to Novi and how they affect equipment life, so we can tell you honestly whether your system has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Tune-Ups That Keep Your System Running

A Novi HVAC maintenance visit is far more than a quick filter swap. Our technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, replaces the filter, and checks static pressure in the duct system. Minor faults are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and the equipment keeps running clean and efficient.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with spring cooling tune-ups and fall heating tune-ups for most systems. This is especially important in dusty areas, where dirty condenser coils choke heat rejection and drive up energy use. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your equipment's age, your household's comfort demands, and your local climate.

Think of maintenance as protection for equipment you've already bought. Each tune-up keeps the coils clean, the charge correct, and the filter fresh, and that lowers monthly energy bills while easing the strain on the compressor and blower. For most households, a plan costs far less than replacing the system ahead of schedule.

Right-Sized Systems

Equipment Sized for Your Actual House

Proper sizing is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before quoting new equipment, Novi HVAC measures the details that drive your home's heating and cooling load: square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout. That gives you a system that runs in long, efficient cycles, controls humidity, and holds every room at the temperature you set.

Picking a new system is about more than efficiency ratings. Our technician will compare heat pumps against a furnace-and-AC pair, and ducted against ductless, laying out operating costs, electrical and venting work, and the installation itself. Growing families often do best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment, which holds tighter temperatures from room to room and uses less energy. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

The HVAC Repairs Novi Homes Need Most

No cooling, a furnace that refuses to light, a frozen evaporator coil, a slow refrigerant leak: each of these problems has a different cause and needs a different repair. Novi HVAC tracks down the exact fault before swapping any parts, whether it's a condenser that hums without starting, a short-cycling furnace, a thermostat that keeps losing its program, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split throwing error codes.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A weak capacitor only keeps the compressor from starting. A cracked heat exchanger, on the other hand, can leak combustion gases into your living space, and a backed-up condensate drain can quietly ruin a ceiling before anyone notices. Our technicians carry the parts to swap capacitors, contactors, fan motors, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards, seal ductwork that's leaking air into the attic, and recharge refrigerant once a leak has been repaired.
